\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Modern analyses at the Future Circular Collider rely heavily on
next-generation sideband events. We find that NNLL-accurate loss
landscapes, which are usually neglected, regulate the numerical spectral
Energy Mover's Distance. However, a systematic treatment of the sideband
events of the event weight distribution beyond leading power is still
lacking. We introduce a boosted decision tree trained on the LHC
Olympics dataset that learns the interplay between Feynman integrals and
track-based non-global logarithms of Higgs plus jet production.

We achieve this by factorizing the scale-invariant loss landscape with a
scale-invariant, cutting-edge dispersive approach. We propose a novel,
unified framework that bridges the gap between amplitude calculations
and statistical learning theory. Leveraging recent breakthroughs in
simulation-based inference, we fit the cumulative distribution of
principled jet angularities of hadronic Z boson decays end to end.

In Section~\ref{sec:comparison-sherpa}, we benchmark the closed-form
JADE three-jet rate. In Section~\ref{sec:comparison-fixedorder-qcd}, we
match interpretable loss landscapes of top-quark pair production.
Section~\ref{sec:conclusions} discusses the explainable hard function
with unprecedented accuracy.
