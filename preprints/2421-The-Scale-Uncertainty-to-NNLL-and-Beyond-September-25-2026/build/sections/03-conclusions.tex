\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}

A discriminative cusp anomalous dimension emerges at order $\alpha_s^3$.
The resulting collinear anomaly agrees with zero-bin subtractions for
all values of $z_{\rm cut}$. Numerically, the pretrained real-virtual
correction differs from the so-called positive hard function by less
than a part in a thousand in the absence of pileup. Extending the method
beyond next-to-leading order is conceptually straightforward but
technically demanding.
