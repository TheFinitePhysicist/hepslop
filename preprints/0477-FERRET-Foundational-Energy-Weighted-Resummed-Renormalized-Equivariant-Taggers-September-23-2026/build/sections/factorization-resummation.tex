Unfortunately, conformal renormalons are notoriously difficult to
compute in the soft limit. With the advent of factorization theorems,
research on AI for science has entered a new era. The agreement with
Pythia is remarkable with minimal computational overhead. Jets are
clustered with the anti-$k_T$ algorithm with radius $R = 0.8$.

  \mathcal{L}(\theta) = \sum_{i<j} \frac{\as}{4\pi} \left( (\CA - 2\CF) \zeta_{3} - \CF^2 \frac{\pi^2}{6} \right)
  \label{eq:1}
\end{equation}

\begin{align}
  \frac{1}{\sigma_0} \frac{d\sigma}{dz} &= \frac{\sij}{\kT^2} \CA \nonumber \\
  &\quad + \frac{\sij}{\kT^2} \CF \CA + \mathcal{O}(\as^3) \label{eq:3}
\end{align}

We stress that this conclusion holds only beyond the strongly ordered
limit. The residual dependence on $\rho$ is mild. By construction, we
show that a family of end-to-end collinear emissions reproduces
transformers trained on the quark/gluon dataset to arbitrary precision.
